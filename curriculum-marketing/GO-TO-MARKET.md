## 0. The honest read

You do not have a content problem. You have a packaging problem.

Sixty hours of facilitator-ready, evidence-cited curriculum is finished and
uniform — that is the expensive part, and it's done. What's missing is
everything between "the content exists" and "a buyer can give you money":
no catalog, no price, no proposal, no evaluation instrument, no proof of
delivery, no license terms. Right now these tracks cannot be bought even by
someone who wants them.

That's good news. The remaining work is roughly two weeks of packaging, not
another six months of writing.

**One credibility leak to fix first:** every facilitator guide footer ends with
`nomadconsulting7@gmail.com`. A gmail address with a trailing digit on a
document you hand to a board chair or a program officer undercuts a $15,000
proposal. Domain email is the single cheapest credibility upgrade available.

## 1. Positioning

### The differentiator you already built and aren't claiming

Most leadership training in this market is uncited opinion in a nice template.
Yours cites Gallup, Google's Project Oxygen, Rogelberg on 1:1s, Edmondson on
psychological safety, Locke & Latham on goal-setting, BoardSource on fiduciary
duty, Tervalon & Murray-Garcia on cultural humility.

More telling: in *Foundations of People Management*, slide 14 openly tells
participants that the scholarly support for Situational Leadership II's strict
matching claims is **thinner than its popularity suggests** — and then names what
the research does firmly support. Almost nobody in this market does that. That
intellectual honesty is your moat, because it is exactly what a skeptical
executive director or a foundation program officer is scanning for.

### Positioning statement

> Leadership and nonprofit capacity training that cites its sources — and tells
> you where the evidence is thin. Culturally responsive by design, not as an
> add-on module.

### Three proof points to repeat everywhere

1. **Evidence-based, and honest about it.** Every module ships with an Evidence
   Base section. We name the limits of the frameworks we teach.
2. **Culturally responsive in the facilitation, not just the slides.** Cultural
   humility is built into the run sheet, the WATCH FOR cues, and the accessibility
   notes — not quarantined in one DEI session.
3. **Practice over theory.** Every module ends with written 30/90-day commitments
   and two take-home tools designed to live next to a keyboard.

### What to stop saying

Drop "training" as the lead noun where you can. You sell **capacity** to
nonprofits and **manager performance** to employers. Training is the delivery
mechanism, not the outcome.

## 2. Product architecture — turning 15 workshops into sellable SKUs

The same 60 files should be sold four different ways at four different price
points. Build the ladder in this order.

### Tier 1 — Facilitated delivery (you in the room) · *build first*

Highest margin per hour, fastest to first revenue, and it generates the
testimonials every other tier depends on.

| SKU | What it is | Nonprofit price | Corporate / government price |
|-----|-----------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| Single half-day workshop | One module, delivered | $2,500 – $4,500 | $6,000 – $10,000 |
| Board retreat session | Module NM-01, delivered to a full board | $3,500 – $6,000 | n/a |
| Track cohort | Full 7- or 8-module sequence, one module/month | $22,000 – $45,000 | $50,000 – $90,000 |
| Custom 3-module intensive | Client picks 3 modules across tracks | $9,000 – $15,000 | $20,000 – $30,000 |

Price on **organizational budget and headcount**, not on your prep time. The prep
is already sunk. Do not discount below $2,500 for a half-day — a low anchor is
very hard to walk back, and in this market a low price is read as low quality.

### Tier 2 — Licensed / train-the-trainer · *build second*

The scaling tier. A client organization buys the right to run the curriculum
internally with their own staff facilitators.

- **Single-module internal license:** $3,000 – $6,000, one org, one year
- **Full-track internal license:** $12,000 – $25,000, one org, one year
- **Train-the-trainer certification:** license + 2-day certification of their
  facilitators, $18,000 – $35,000
- **Annual renewal:** 25–30% of initial license (this is the recurring revenue)

Licensing requires terms of use, which you do not currently have. See §5.

### Tier 3 — Self-serve digital kits · *build third*

Passive revenue and a credibility artifact. Each kit = slides + facilitator guide
+ both handouts, delivered as a download.

- **Single module kit:** $249 – $399
- **Full track kit (7 or 8 modules):** $1,200 – $1,900
- **Both tracks:** $2,400

Sell on your own site with Stripe. Do **not** launch on a marketplace that takes
50% and owns the customer relationship. These are consulting lead generators as
much as products.

### Tier 4 — Free lead magnets · *build alongside Tier 3*

Release exactly two handouts free, gated behind an email address:

- **Nonprofit track:** *Three Duties Fluency Card and Governance Sort* (NM-01) —
  every board chair in America has quietly wondered whether they understand their
  legal duties. This is the highest-intent download you own.
- **Leadership track:** *One-on-One and Feedback Planner* (LM-01) — high utility,
  immediately usable, and it name-drops the Rogelberg research.

Never gate more than two. The free tier is a sample, not a substitute.

## 3. Who actually buys — ranked by dollars per hour of your effort

### Tier A: Intermediaries and funders (highest leverage — start here)

These buyers purchase **on behalf of many organizations at once**. One sale
serves 12–30 nonprofits and produces 12–30 testimonials.

- **Community foundations** running grantee capacity-building programs
- **State nonprofit associations** (every state has one; most run annual
  conferences and member training calendars)
- **United Way chapters** with agency capacity programs
- **Private foundations** with explicit capacity-building or organizational
  effectiveness portfolios
- **Fiscal sponsors** managing portfolios of small nonprofits
- **Nonprofit management support organizations** and CVNL-type centers

The pitch to a funder is not "buy training." It is: *your grantees keep failing
on governance and financial management; here is a 60-hour curriculum you can
fund as a cohort for your portfolio, with pre/post measurement.*

### Tier B: Direct organizational buyers

- Nonprofits with 15+ staff and a real professional development line item
- Hospital systems, universities, and health systems with community benefit funds
- Municipal and county government L&D departments (Leadership track)
- Associations and membership organizations serving nonprofit professionals

### Tier C: Individual buyers (Tier 3 digital only)

- Newly promoted managers paying out of pocket or with a small L&D stipend
- Independent consultants who want a curriculum to deliver under license
- Executive directors of small orgs who will facilitate it themselves

**Do not** build a marketing plan around Tier C. It is the lowest-value buyer and
the highest-support one. Tier C exists to feed your email list and validate
demand, not to pay your mortgage.

## 4. Packaging work — the blocking list

Nothing in §6 can start until these exist. This is roughly 10–14 working days.

### Must-have before any sales conversation (Week 1)

| # | Deliverable | Why it blocks | Effort |
|---|-------------|---------------|--------|
| P1 | **Domain email + basic brand kit** (logo, 2 colors, 2 fonts) | Gmail address on a $15k proposal kills deals | 0.5 day |
| P2 | **Two-track capability one-pager** (PDF) | The thing you attach to every cold email | 1 day |
| P3 | **Course catalog** — 15 modules, each with purpose, audience, objectives, length, take-home tools | Buyers compare catalogs; you have none | 2 days |
| P4 | **Pricing sheet** (internal) + **rate card** (external) | You cannot quote consistently without one | 0.5 day |
| P5 | **Pre/post evaluation instrument** | Your own facilitator guides tell you to collect it. Funders require it. Its absence is the biggest single gap. | 1 day |
| P6 | **Proposal / SOW template** | Every Tier A and B sale needs one | 1 day |

### Must-have before licensing or digital sales (Week 2)

| # | Deliverable | Why | Effort |
|---|-------------|-----|--------|
| P7 | **Compiled participant workbook per track** (all handouts, branded, paginated) | Turns loose files into a product people photograph and share | 2 days |
| P8 | **Client-ready PDF exports** of all 15 decks and guides | Never send an editable `.pptx` to a client | 0.5 day |
| P9 | **Terms of use / license agreement** | Required for Tier 2. Without it you are licensing on a handshake. | 1 day (get a lawyer's eyes on it) |
| P10 | **Certificate of completion template** | Cheap; participants post them on LinkedIn; free distribution | 0.5 day |
| P11 | **Landing page per track** with Stripe checkout | Nothing to link to right now | 2 days |

### Should-have (Weeks 3–4, can run parallel to selling)

| # | Deliverable | Why |
|---|-------------|-----|
| P12 | **CEU / credential pathway** — approach SHRM and CFRE for pre-approval | Materially raises willingness to pay, and it is a differentiator competitors won't bother with |
| P13 | **Two case studies** from pilot deliveries | Proof beats claims |
| P14 | **Facilitator bio one-pager** | Buyers buy the facilitator as much as the curriculum |
| P15 | **Virtual delivery adaptation notes** | Guides assume an in-person room. Half your market will ask for Zoom. |

**On P12:** SHRM recertification provider status (Leadership track) and CFRE
continuing education points (Fundraising and Grant Writing modules) are worth
real money. A workshop that carries CEUs justifies a 20–40% price premium and
gives the buyer's L&D department a reason to approve the spend.

**On P15:** every facilitator guide currently specifies flip charts, name tents,
pairs seated in trios, and a physical break slide. That is a real constraint you
should decide about deliberately — either you sell in-person-only as a premium
positioning choice, or you write the virtual adaptation. Don't discover this on a
sales call.

## 5. Intellectual property and licensing

You have no license terms on 60 files that are about to be distributed. Fix
before Tier 2 or Tier 3 launches.

- Add a copyright line and use restriction to every slide master and guide footer
- Decide the license unit: per organization, per facilitator, or per cohort
- Decide the renewal term (annual is standard)
- Trademark search "Nomad Consulting" before you spend on brand assets
- **Third-party frameworks:** you teach SLII (Blanchard), GROW, SBI (Center for
  Creative Leadership), and Kotter. Teaching and citing a published framework is
  normal and fine. Using a trademarked name as a *product* name is not. Keep
  framework names inside the content, out of your SKU names. Have counsel confirm
  before you sell Tier 2 licenses at scale.

## 6. Marketing plan — first 90 days

### Days 1–14 — Packaging sprint

Execute P1–P6. Do not market anything yet. Sell nothing you cannot deliver a
proposal for within 24 hours.

### Days 15–30 — Manufacture proof

You need testimonials more than you need traffic.

1. **Deliver two pilot workshops at cost or free**, deliberately chosen:
   - One board governance session (NM-01) with a well-connected local nonprofit
   - One Foundations of People Management (LM-01) with a mid-size employer
2. Run the P5 evaluation instrument at both. Capture pre/post numbers.
3. Get **video testimonial + written quote + permission to name the org** — ask
   before delivery, not after.
4. Write both up as case studies (P13).

The price for a free pilot is not zero. It is a testimonial, a case study, and
three warm introductions. Say that out loud when you offer it.

### Days 31–60 — Outbound to Tier A, content engine on

**Outbound (the revenue driver — 60% of your marketing time):**

- Build a list of 40 Tier A targets: state nonprofit associations, community
  foundations with capacity-building portfolios, United Way chapters in your region
- Personalized email to each: one paragraph on a problem their grantees have, the
  capability one-pager attached, one specific ask (a 20-minute call)
- Target: 40 sent, 8–10 conversations, 2–3 proposals, 1 closed cohort
- Follow up three times. Most of this market replies on contact two or three.

**Conference and speaking track (highest-ROI channel in this sector):**

- State nonprofit association annual conferences — submit sessions now; CFPs run
  4–8 months ahead
- AFP (Association of Fundraising Professionals) chapter meetings — monthly, always
  need speakers, and the room is full of exactly your buyer
- Regional grantmaker association meetings

A conference session is a paid or free 90-minute infomercial delivered to 60
qualified buyers, with the association's endorsement attached. Treat every
speaking slot as lead generation: always have a handout with a QR code to the
lead magnet.

**Content engine (20% of time — you already have the machinery):**

You already have a `yap-reel-generator` skill configured for exactly these topics
and an existing content pattern in Notion (the Nonprofit Sustainability Blueprint
launch, LinkedIn posts, newsletter). Point it at the curriculum.

- **LinkedIn, 3×/week.** Mine the Evidence Base sections — they are 15 modules'
  worth of citable, contrarian, specific posts. High-performing angles you already
  own: *"Gallup found manager engagement hit a record low of 22% in 2025"*;
  *"vague personality critique in reviews lands hardest on women and people of
  color — here's the fix"*; *"the governance/management line is a leading cause of
  ED turnover."*
- **Reels/Shorts, 2×/week** via the existing skill.
- **Monthly newsletter** to the list built from lead magnets.

Post the honest caveats too. "Here's a framework everyone teaches, and here's
where the research doesn't actually back it" is the single most shareable thing
you have.

### Days 61–90 — Launch cohort + digital tier

- **Open enrollment for one public cohort** of the Nonprofit track — 12–20 seats
  at $1,800–$2,800/seat, monthly half-days. A public cohort de-risks the buyer
  (they don't have to commit a whole org) and creates the case study for the
  organizational sale.
- Launch Tier 3 digital kits (P11 landing pages live)
- Ship P12 CEU applications

## 7. Which track to lead with

**Lead with the Nonprofit Management and Capacity Track.** Reasons:

1. Tier A intermediary buyers exist for it and have dedicated capacity-building
   budgets. The leadership market has no equivalent concentrated buyer.
2. It is less crowded. Corporate leadership training is a commodity ocean;
   nonprofit capacity building has far fewer credible, evidence-cited providers.
3. Board Governance Essentials is a natural door-opener — it is the session
   nonprofits know they need, it's delivered to the board (the people who approve
   budgets), and its facilitator guide already flags a 90-minute self-paced
   onboarding derivative you can sell as a recurring add-on.
4. It matches the audience your existing content and Notion pipeline already
   speak to.

Sell the Leadership track **into the same accounts as an expansion**, plus
opportunistically to corporate and government buyers where margins are 2–3×.
